Distribution Platform v2 — Requirements
Specification
Oct 8, 2026  ·  @Claude
1. Purpose and scope
This document defines the target system that replaces the current single-level dispatch
prototype: a demand-driven distribution platform for packaged goods, run on a daily
ordering and delivery cycle, with dairy in Chennai as the first product profile.
What the system does, end to end
1 . Forecasts tomorrow's demand per booth and product with an ML model.
2. Sends each booth a suggested order that auto-confirms unless the booth changes it
before the cut-off.
3. Rolls booth orders up into each hub's order to the plant.
4. Allocates stock fairly when supply is short, at both levels.
5. Plans trucks from plant to hubs and vans from hubs to booths.
6. Runs deliveries live: tracking, disruptions, urgent requests, proof of delivery, crates.
7. Closes the day and stores forecast vs actual for every date.
In scope: outbound distribution of packed products, plant → hubs → booths, with web
dashboards for managers and three mobile apps (booth ordering, hub ordering, driver).
Out of scope for this version: raw milk collection, plant production, doorstep delivery,
payments (see section 22).
Product name. The name comes from configuration ( APP_NAME ). Working proposal:
Allocra. Dairy is the first profile; amoxicillin is the second profile planned on the same
engine.
How to read this document. Sections 3–6 define the world (network, products, vehicles,
people). Sections 7–13 define the daily workflow. Sections 14–16 define screens, time and
ML. Sections 17–20 are for implementation. Sections 21–24 cover the demo, limits and
build plan. Words like must are requirements; should are strong defaults; may are optional.

Nodes
Plant: the single source. Packs products into crates and holds plant stock per SKU.
Default: Madhavaram, Chennai.
Hub: a depot that receives crates from the plant and serves its booths. Has its own
vans, a hub manager (web) and a hub ordering user (app). Default: 7 hubs across
Chennai.
Booth: a retail point (booth, parlour, shop, distributor) that orders from its hub and
receives one delivery per cycle. Default: 8–12 per hub.
Rules (must)
Every booth belongs to exactly one hub; the assignment is set in the network editor or
CSV.
A hub's vans only serve that hub's booths.
Exception: a hub manager may lend a booth to a neighbouring hub for one day; the
other hub manager accepts; both plans update; it is logged.
Hub → booth dispatch cannot start until that hub has received its plant delivery (or the
manager overrides with stock on hand).
Why a tree and not many-to-many: each booth has one supplier and one route in real
distribution, which keeps planning fast and explainable. Several plants serving one city,
and automatic booth-to-hub clustering, are roadmap items (section 22).
4. Products and packaging
Everything after the plant moves as packed products in crates or cartons; no loose liquid
is carried at any level. Orders are placed in units, planned and loaded in crates, and
reported in litres or kg.
Product (SKU) fields: id, name, category, pack size (ml or g), unit type (pouch, cup, carton,
tin), units per crate or carton, weight per full crate (kg), temperature lane, shelf life (hours
or days), active flag.
Temperature lanes:
Chilled (2–8 °C): milk pouches, curd, buttermilk, paneer, butter.
Ambient: ghee, UHT milk, milk powder, sweets.
Frozen (−18 °C or lower): ice cream. Optional in this version.
A product may only be loaded on a vehicle that supports its lane.

7. Daily cycle
Every delivery day D is prepared the afternoon before: orders lock by 18:00 on D-1, plant
trucks run in the evening, and hub vans deliver overnight so every booth is served by
05:00 on D.
Each event runs exactly once per day, in this order; the prototype clock can jump between
them (section 15).
What each event does
1 . ML suggestions: forecasts written for D; each booth gets its suggested order and
reason (section 8).
2. Booth cut-off: untouched orders auto-confirm; all booth orders lock; pending
approvals wait for the hub manager.
3. Hub default orders: each hub's order to the plant is computed from its booths' final
orders (section 19) and shown in the hub app.
4. Hub cut-off: hub orders lock; the plant allocates stock across hubs if short (section 9);
each hub then allocates its expected receipt across booths.
5. Plant trucks to hubs: trucks are planned and dispatched; hubs confirm receipt in the
hub app.
6. Hub vans to booths: vans are planned per hub, loaded and dispatched; live operations
run until every stop is done (sections 10–12).
daily cycle · D-1 14:00 to D 06:00

7. Day close: vans return with empties and returns; reconciliation is stored as the record
for D; actual demand is recorded for ML.
Dependencies (must): each event waits for the one before it; a hub's vans wait for its
plant delivery; late approvals revert to the default at the next cut-off.
8. Ordering
Orders use an auto-confirmed default: the system proposes a quantity, silence means
yes, and only a change needs action before the cut-off.
8.1 Booth order (booth → hub, in the booth app)
1 . At suggestion time the booth receives tomorrow's suggested order per SKU, with a
one-line reason (e.g. "usual Friday · Deepavali in 2 days · rain expected").
2. Until the booth cut-off the booth may: leave it (it auto-confirms), change any quantity
with + / − or by typing, or mark Closed tomorrow (order becomes zero).
3. A change beyond ±30 % of the suggestion for a SKU needs hub manager approval and
a reason; smaller changes need no approval and the reason is optional.
4. 30 minutes before the cut-off the app reminds the booth: "Your order auto-confirms at
16:00."
5. At the cut-off the order locks. Pending approvals not decided by the hub cut-off revert
to the suggestion.
Booth order states: Suggested → Changed / Pending approval → Confirmed (auto or
manual) → Locked → Allocated (allocated vs ordered, with reason if short) → Out for
delivery (van ETA) → Delivered (booth confirms crates, empties, damage) → Closed.
8.2 Hub order (hub → plant, in the hub app)
1 . After the booth cut-off the system computes the hub's default order per SKU (formula
in section 19) and shows its breakdown: booth orders, buffer, usable stock at the hub.
2. Until the hub cut-off the hub may leave it (auto-confirms) or change it. A change
beyond ±20 % needs plant manager approval and a reason.
3. At the hub cut-off the order locks and goes to plant allocation.
Hub order states: Computed → Changed / Pending approval → Confirmed → Locked →
Allocated → In transit (truck ETA) → Received (hub confirms crates and shortages) →
Closed.

9. Shortage allocation
When supply of a SKU is less than total orders, the same rule runs at both levels: the plant
shares its stock across hubs at the hub cut-off, and each hub shares its expected receipt
across booths. Nobody finds out at the counter: every receiver sees allocated vs ordered
and the reason before dispatch.
Rule, per SKU (configurable):
1 . Remove closed receivers.
2. Guaranteed share: each receiver gets min(order, minimum share × order), default
minimum share 80 %, served in priority order Critical → High → Normal. If even
guaranteed shares don't fit, Critical receivers are filled first, then the rest in proportion
to their orders.
3. Remainder: what is left is split in proportion to each receiver's unmet order.
4. Round to whole units, then to crates for loading; rounding leftovers go to the receiver
with the largest unmet share.
Settings: minimum share %, priority per hub and per booth (with a reason, e.g. hospital
nearby), optional channel rule (e.g. booths before bulk or online orders).
Outputs: allocation table per SKU (ordered, allocated, short, %), a notification to each
affected booth or hub, and a decision log entry with the rule applied.

10. Planning and dispatch
One planning engine serves both levels; only the source, receivers and vehicles change.
The engine sits behind a Planner interface so the current heuristic can be replaced by OR-
Tools later without touching anything else.
Planner input: source (plant or hub), receivers with allocated crates per SKU and delivery
windows, vehicles available at that source, products (lanes, weights), road distances and
times, dispatch start time.
Planner output: trips; each trip = vehicle, driver, ordered stops, crates per SKU per stop,
ETA and slack per stop, km, fuel, cost, utilisation.
Hard constraints (must never be broken):
Crates and kg on a vehicle within capacity.
Each product only on a vehicle supporting its lane; a receiver needing two lanes may
get two vehicles.
Every allocated crate on exactly one trip.
Trips start after dispatch time and end within the vehicle shift.
A van's crates must be available at its hub (received from the plant, or overridden by
the manager using stock on hand).
Objective (in strict order): serve all allocated crates → meet delivery windows → fewest
vehicles → least km.
Loading sheet per trip: crates per SKU per stop, loaded in reverse stop order (last stop in
first); printable and shown in the driver app.

11.4 Decision log

and reason, and is visible in the web views.

16. ML forecasting and synthetic data
No real booth-level sales data is available, so the system generates realistic synthetic
history from public patterns, trains on it, and labels every chart and number that depends
on it "Synthetic data". The method is real; the accuracy figures describe the method, not
real booths.
16.1 Synthetic data
Public patterns to research and cite (in docs/DATA_SOURCES.md ): weekday variation
in packaged milk sales; Tamil Nadu festival and holiday dates for last and current year
(Pongal, Tamil New Year, Vinayagar Chathurthi, Deepavali and others); Chennai monthly
rainfall and temperature normals (IMD or similar).
Generator, per booth × SKU × day, 365 days: base level by booth type (residential,
commercial, near market) × product mix × weekday factor × festival effect (pre-
festival rise, festival-day pattern per SKU, e.g. curd and ghee up) × rain effect ×
temperature effect (buttermilk up in heat) × slow trend × random noise.
All parameters in one config file; fixed random seed; sample plots saved for a sanity
check.
When a simulated day closes, its actual demand comes from the same generator, so
forecast vs actual is always available.
16.2 Model
One global gradient-boosting model (LightGBM) across all booths and SKUs.

Hub default order (per SKU)
\text{hub order} = \max\Big(0,\; \sum_{\text{booths}} \text{final order} + z

Spare crates per van (per SKU): spare = round(z_van × √ Σ  (p90 − p50)² over the van's

Approval limits: booth change |final − suggestion| / suggestion > 30 % → approval; hub
change > 20 % → approval.
Shortage allocation: guaranteed min(order, 80 % × order) by priority, remainder in
proportion to unmet orders (section 9).
